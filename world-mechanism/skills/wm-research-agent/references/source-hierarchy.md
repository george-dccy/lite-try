# Source Hierarchy

## Tier 1 — Original / Primary
Examples:
- law / regulation / court filing;
- original paper;
- official dataset;
- company technical documentation;
- archival document;
- transcript / original interview;
- historical object record.

Use for:
- dates;
- exact wording;
- original methods;
- legal / institutional status;
- product claims.

## Tier 2 — Domain Authority Synthesis
Examples:
- museum or archive research essay;
- systematic review;
- standards body explanation;
- major professional institution.

Use for:
- context;
- consensus;
- connecting multiple primary facts.

## Tier 3 — High-quality Secondary
Examples:
- specialist journalism;
- expert history;
- technical explainers with transparent sourcing.

Use for:
- discovering primary sources;
- contested interpretations;
- accessible synthesis.

## Tier 4 — Community / Social
Examples:
- Reddit;
- forums;
- comments;
- anecdotal creator experiences.

Use for:
- lived experience;
- language people use;
- problem discovery;
- hypotheses.

Do not use Tier 4 alone for a load-bearing factual claim.

## Source independence

Five articles repeating the same press release are one evidence family, not five independent confirmations.

## First-party caution

First-party sources are strongest for:
- what a product says it does;
- current feature description;
- company-published metrics.

They are not automatically strongest for:
- whether the product is socially beneficial;
- causal claims outside the study design;
- independent assessment of harms.

## Historical caution

Later summaries can compress sequences.
Always check:
- what happened first;
- when practice spread;
- when law formalized it;
- whether later terminology existed at the time.
