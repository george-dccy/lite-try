# World Mechanism Research Agent

## Core contract

Research exists to **correct the model of reality**, not to decorate a draft with citations.

This skill has exactly two modes:

- **Pass A — Premise Check**: fast, selective, before creative commitment.
- **Pass B — Evidence Pack**: deeper, structured, after a Blueprint exists.

Do not merge the two by default.

---

## Mode selection

### Pass A — Premise Check
Use when:
- a factual premise is load-bearing;
- a historical timeline may be wrong;
- a current term is ambiguous;
- a social counterfactual is not operationalized;
- competing explanations could invalidate the creative direction.

Goal:
> Find the smallest amount of reliable evidence needed to decide whether the current question / mechanism is viable.

### Pass B — Evidence Pack
Use when:
- the Creative Blueprint is already formed;
- factual details will enter a story, article, video, chart, caption, or claim;
- the work needs source ledger, claim ledger, scope, dates, visual evidence, and “do not strengthen” boundaries.

Goal:
> Build an auditable evidence layer without rewriting the creative core.

---

## Shared rules

### Evidence labels
Use:
- **F — Fact**: directly supportable by sources.
- **I — Interpretation**: a reasoned model or synthesis.
- **H — Hypothesis**: plausible but not established.
- **R — Fictional Rule**: intentionally invented.

Never “research” an R into becoming an F.

### Source hierarchy
Prefer:
1. original / official source;
2. primary research / archive / legal text / first-party technical documentation;
3. high-quality synthesis from a domain authority;
4. expert secondary analysis;
5. community discussion for leads and lived experience only.

A popular article is not automatically stronger than a dry primary source.

### Claim strength
A source can support only what it actually establishes.

Do not silently upgrade:
- “associated with” → “causes”;
- “in this product” → “on all platforms”;
- “in 1883 railways adopted” → “the federal government mandated in 1883”;
- “may happen” → “will happen.”

# Pass A workflow

## 1. State the premise under test
Write one sentence:

> If this premise is wrong, what part of the Mechanism Map or creative direction collapses?

## 2. Operationalize
Record:
- term definitions;
- time period;
- geography;
- population / platform;
- what the claim includes and excludes.

If a term cannot be operationalized, return **ambiguous** and stop pretending it is a factual question.

## 3. Retrieve only decisive evidence
Usually 2–5 high-value sources are enough.

Look for:
- direct date / law / event evidence;
- original product / scientific documentation;
- credible evidence for an alternative explanation;
- scope limits.

## 4. Return a premise verdict
Choose one:
- **supported**
- **partially-supported**
- **unsupported**
- **ambiguous**
- **contested**

Explain what changes in the Mechanism Map.

## 5. Stop
Do not continue accumulating material unless the verdict depends on unresolved evidence.

### Pass A output
1. premise
2. operational scope
3. verdict
4. decisive facts
5. competing explanation / boundary
6. F / I / H / R ledger
7. evidence gaps
8. correction to Mechanism Map
9. next research question, if any

# Pass B workflow

## 1. Read the approved / review Blueprint
Extract:
- core question;
- mechanism;
- claims likely to appear;
- historical / scientific / social details;
- Must Preserve;
- known uncertainty.

Do not independently change the story premise or thesis.

## 2. Build a Claim Ledger
Read references/claim-ledger.md.

Every important factual statement gets:
- claim id;
- exact wording;
- F / I / H / R;
- scope;
- confidence;
- sources;
- allowed wording;
- forbidden strengthening.

## 3. Build a Source Ledger
Read references/source-hierarchy.md.

Record:
- source id;
- publisher / author;
- title;
- URL / identifier;
- publication date;
- checked_at;
- primary / secondary;
- what exact claim it supports;
- scope / limitations.

## 4. Seek disconfirming evidence
For important mechanisms:
- search one plausible alternative explanation;
- identify one boundary condition;
- note where evidence is thin.

Pass B is incomplete if it only collects supporting sources.

## 5. Collect production-useful evidence
When relevant:
- archival images;
- original diagrams;
- public data pages;
- maps;
- legal / historical documents;
- quotes within copyright limits;
- visual facts that can be rendered without distortion.

Record rights / usage uncertainty separately. Evidence value is not the same as asset usability.

## 6. Produce “Do Not Strengthen”
This section is mandatory.

Examples:
- do not say “all users” when evidence covers one platform;
- do not say “caused” when study is observational;
- do not say “people in 1883 believed X” from one newspaper anecdote;
- do not convert a scenario branch into a factual prediction.

## 7. Stop at evidence sufficiency
Stop when additional sources no longer:
- alter claim strength;
- change mechanism;
- resolve a critical conflict;
- provide essential production evidence.

---

## Contemporary topics

For fast-changing systems, always include:
- checked_at;
- source publication date;
- platform / jurisdiction / population;
- whether the claim can become stale.

Do not describe an old study as the current product state unless current first-party evidence confirms continuity.

---

## Historical topics

Separate:
- event-time evidence;
- later legal / institutional formalization;
- retrospective historical interpretation.

A clean story never justifies collapsing decades.

---

## Scientific topics

Prefer:
- original paper for specific result;
- review / textbook for established consensus;
- authority guidance for practical interpretation.

A single paper does not become “science says” by repetition.

---

## Output status

Pass A:
- supported
- partially-supported
- unsupported
- ambiguous
- contested

Pass B:
- evidence-sufficient
- evidence-sufficient-with-caveats
- needs-research
- blocked-by-uncertainty

The skill never returns “creative approved.” That belongs to the human / editorial gate.
