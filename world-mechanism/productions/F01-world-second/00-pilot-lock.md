# F01 Pilot Production Lock

## Purpose

F01 is now the **first production pilot** for the World Mechanism Story Engine.

The immediate goal is not to turn F01 into the definitive flagship film. The goal is to prove that the engine can move a sufficiently deep idea through:

`Mechanism → Creative → Content Master → Video Concept → Director Proposal → Production Manifest → Codex/art-motion → rough cut → QA → finished short video`

with limited manual re-planning.

## Pilot target

- Duration: **90–110 seconds**
- Form: **embodied first person**
- Visual direction for this pilot: **unified contemporary illustration**
- Main production system: **Codex + art-motion**
- Optional local dynamic-asset backend: **MiniMax H3 Lite**
- Immediate milestone: **first rough cut**

> **Scope note:** the unified contemporary illustration choice is a production decision for F01 only. It is not a project-wide default and must not be inherited by later films without a new style-routing decision.

## What is locked

The Content Master and cognitive structure are no longer open for routine rewriting before first rough cut.

The film must preserve this full chain:

1. I live one normal, continuous morning.
2. At `08:00:00`, normal time freezes and my question becomes an ability.
3. I first try to experience all simultaneous lives at once.
4. That removes missing-out but destroys foreground attention and context.
5. I then enter lives one by one and discover that a meaningful second has its own before/after context.
6. Sequential replay restores context but cannot fit the world's one second inside one person's time.
7. AI summary solves capacity but separates knowing from experiencing.
8. Curating only the best moments creates excellent fragments, not one continuous life.
9. I return to my still-frozen `08:00:00`.
10. Other lives remain real and complete, but were never pieces removed from mine.
11. My unfinished action resumes and `08:00:00` becomes `08:00:01`.

This is the depth layer. First-person form must make it more experiential, not simpler.

## What may change before first rough cut

Allowed:
- shot design;
- duration compression;
- exact ordinary-life examples;
- sparse first-person wording;
- blocking and body cues;
- camera implementation;
- style-bible details;
- H3 Lite vs code-drawn asset ratio;
- transitions;
- sound design;
- technical implementation.

Not allowed by default:
- replacing the four-step Wish Fulfillment chain;
- adding a new thesis;
- returning to the cup/repetition thesis;
- reframing the ending as regret, fate, "ordinary life is best", "don't envy others", or "cherish the present";
- extending the project because a new philosophical sentence sounds better.

## One-change rule

Between this lock and first rough cut:

> **At most one structural revision is allowed.**

It must be triggered by an actual failed prototype or rough-cut problem, not by speculative polishing.

The log must state:
1. what failed on screen;
2. why shot/timing/sound changes cannot fix it;
3. which upstream structure must change;
4. the smallest change needed;
5. the re-test result.

## First-person rule

First-person means **the audience experiences the mechanism from inside the protagonist's temporal position**.

It does not require a videogame-style camera and it does not mean continuous narration.

Use:
- gaze;
- hands;
- footsteps;
- breath;
- shadow;
- reflection;
- body-near sound;
- subjective spatial continuity.

Speech should be sparse. Visual Proof beats should be allowed to operate without explanation.

## Visual system

For **F01 specifically**, style variation is not the primary cognitive proof.

Use one unified contemporary illustration universe and let the following change instead:
- number of worlds;
- amount of context;
- information density;
- spatial sound;
- window grammar;
- continuity.

This makes this particular pilot a cleaner test of the mechanism and is compatible with economical local generation.

This is **not** a reusable default for later productions. Every new film must return to `wm-video-producer` style routing and choose its visual strategy from the content itself. Valid future choices include, among others:
- one consistent style;
- several sectional styles;
- simultaneous parallel-world styles;
- live-action / realistic treatment;
- collage / archive / whiteboard / information graphics;
- Chinese ink or other culturally specific visual languages;
- mixed code-drawn and generated media;
- short deliberate style-speedrun passages.

A later production should inherit F01's style only if its own cognitive and dramatic needs independently justify it.

## H3 Lite boundary

H3 Lite is permitted as a bounded dynamic-asset backend after Style Bible lock.

Good uses:
- 4–8 second living-motion clips inside parallel-world windows;
- human movement that is disproportionately costly to code;
- environmental motion that benefits from generative dynamics.

Codex/art-motion must retain ownership of:
- master timeline;
- time freeze;
- window multiplication;
- long-scroll structure;
- information phase;
- transitions;
- typography;
- audio cues;
- final `08:00:00 → 08:00:01` proof;
- QA.

## First rough cut definition

The first rough cut is successful if a viewer can follow, without a philosophical explanation track:

> one normal life → impossible access to many lives → simultaneous overload → context restored but queue becomes endless → summary knows but does not live → best moments remain fragments → return to my own line → next second.

It does not need final polish.

## Stop condition

Once the first rough cut is coherent enough to review, stop polishing F01 at the system-design level.

Log lessons, fix only the most important production failures, then move the engine to the next pilot cases.
